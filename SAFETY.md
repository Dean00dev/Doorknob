# Safety boundary

Doorknob's bounded purpose is to help a person organise their own account before
a medical appointment. It must not cross into diagnosis, treatment, triage, or
clearance to wait.

## Load-bearing rules

1. Emergency information is visible before the interview and throughout it.
2. The person explicitly chooses appointment preparation before starting.
3. A model's urgent flag is supplementary only and is described that way.
4. A missing urgent flag never produces reassurance.
5. Users review and can edit every answer before a brief is generated.
6. The named worry comes directly from the reviewed worry answer.
7. Text labelled as an exact quotation must occur in the reviewed transcript.
8. Every generated brief is labelled as a draft requiring human correction.
9. Printing a brief retains emergency, limitation, privacy, and draft-warning
   context.

## Known limitations

- Free text can describe an emergency in unlimited ways. No keyword list,
  prompt, or language model is complete enough to be a triage guarantee.
- A model can omit, distort, or over-emphasise information.
- The mock provider demonstrates mechanics, not medical usefulness.
- The UK emergency wording is not localisation for other countries.
- There has been no clinician review, user study, clinical-risk assessment,
  medical-device determination, or accessibility audit.

## Advancement rule

Do not describe Doorknob as detecting emergencies, improving clinical outcomes,
or producing clinician-ready records without predetermined tests and appropriate
independent clinical, accessibility, legal, and data-protection review.
